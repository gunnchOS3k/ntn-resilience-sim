# PhD Research Role

## Purpose

This repository studies how NTN-style conditions—high latency, intermittent availability, and limited throughput—affect service continuity for different workload profiles.

## Research Scope

The research investigates degradation patterns and adaptation strategies when terrestrial connectivity is disrupted and non-terrestrial network (NTN) fallback becomes the primary or only available path. Workload profiles span emergency services, rural connectivity, disaster response, humanitarian operations, and polar/remote sensing.

## Infrastructure Requirements

This research does **not** require live 6G or satellite access. Published specifications and modeled parameters from 3GPP NTN standards, ITU recommendations, and peer-reviewed literature are sufficient to construct representative simulation environments.

## Scientific Contribution

This is core scientific infrastructure for the PhD, providing:

- A reproducible simulation framework for NTN resilience evaluation
- Scenario-driven analysis of service continuity under constrained connectivity
- Quantitative comparison of adaptation strategies against defined baselines
- Evidence of how workload characteristics interact with NTN link properties

## Methodology

All experiments use modeled network parameters derived from published specifications. Results characterize expected behavior under simulated NTN conditions and do not represent measurements from operational satellite or HAPS systems.
